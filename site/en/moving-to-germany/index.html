      Taking your belongings to Germany

      What goes with you across the border?

      A sofa that has to come along, a wardrobe that comes apart and a few personal boxes you want to pack yourself. If you are moving to Germany from the Netherlands, planning starts with an overview of your things. The storage room, attic and garden furniture count too.

      During the pre-move survey we discuss the volume of your belongings and access to both homes. You tell us which jobs you will do yourself and where you would like help. With a video survey you can show us the rooms and larger furniture remotely.

      Do you already have the keys, or is the date still open? Let us know straight away. We can then discuss what can be arranged for collection and delivery. For the rest of your preparations, read more about moving abroad (in Dutch).

      Measure large furniture and the doorways in your new home. A wardrobe that fits through the front door can still get stuck on the stairs. With your quote request, ask about dismantling and reassembly (page in Dutch).

      Things to arrange

      Look outside and upstairs too

      Do not leave the garden furniture, the attic or the storage room off your overview. Include them in the survey so the volume is right. Anything you would rather not take to Germany can be set aside before the survey.

      Measurements from the German end

      No access to your new home yet? Ask the landlord or estate agent for measurements or photos of the stairwell and the lift. That way we can check in advance which pieces will need furniture assembly (in Dutch).

      A list, even within the EU

      Packing a few personal boxes yourself? Tell us what is in them. De Bresser adds the contents to the numbered inventory list, even for a move within the EU, and you receive a copy.

      Preparing for moving day

      When can your things go into the new house?

      Your old home has to be empty, but you may only get the keys to the new one later. Put the two dates side by side. We then discuss collection, transport to Germany and delivery to your address.

      Tell us the floor, whether there is a lift and the distance from the street to the front door. Photos of the entrance and stairwell help us discuss the situation. For a flat, also ask when the lift or shared entrance may be used.

      And the cost? That depends on what you are taking and what work you ask us to do. So check which services, any storage and any costs for access to the address are included in your quote. Keep identity documents, medication and things for the first night close at hand.

      Plan together

      Hand over now, move in later

      Is there a gap between handing over your old home empty and getting the keys in Germany? Give us both dates as early as you can. We will then discuss a later delivery to your new address and container storage (page in Dutch).

      Where will the van park?

      No fixed loading bay at your new home? Tell us what the street looks like and we will agree who applies to the municipality for permission and who puts up the signs. Allow for the processing time.

      A bag you carry yourself

      Set aside ID, medication, chargers and bedding for the first night. Put them in a bag that travels with you. That way they are to hand while the van is still being unloaded.

      Moving in practice

      Space for the removal van in Germany

      The procedure depends on the municipality and on where the van needs to park. Ask your German municipality which permission is needed, who applies and who arranges any signs. The examples below apply only to Berlin and Düsseldorf.

      Municipal information checked on 19 October 2026. These are local examples; the competent authority decides on your application.

      Photo credits

      • Your loading and unloading spot

        Include the details of the loading and unloading spot when you send your request to De Bresser. Agree in writing who arranges any permission and signs. The competent authority decides on the application.

      • Berlin: temporary no-stopping zone

        For a temporary no-stopping zone, you apply for an order from the road traffic authority of the district concerned. The city gives an average processing time of about four weeks. That is no guarantee your application will be ready within four weeks. The authority does not supply the signs; talk to a specialist company about putting them up. Read Berlin's guidance on no-stopping zones for removals (in German).

      • Düsseldorf: exemption from a no-stopping zone

        To park or stop within an existing no-stopping zone, you need an exemption permit. The usual lead time is fourteen days. Since 1 June 2026 a shorter lead time is possible at a higher fee; applying less than two council working days ahead is not possible. The city does not put up the signs. Check Düsseldorf's application conditions (in German) for your situation.

      Customs and arrangements

      Do you need customs papers for Germany?

      Germany is an EU member state. For household goods leaving the Netherlands for Germany, there is therefore generally no customs declaration to make in either country. De Bresser still always draws up an inventory list. We number every item and box and record the contents of each box. You get your own copy, so you can see exactly what is travelling.

      Cars and motorbikes may be subject to extra rules. Report special items separately and check what applies to your goods and route. Dutch Customs explains the rules for moving within the EU (in Dutch). If your goods or route do need customs formalities after all, De Bresser handles them. You supply the necessary details and documents. Registering in Germany, deregistering in the Netherlands and other personal matters are separate. Our guide to emigrating to Germany (in Dutch) is written for Dutch nationals; with another passport, check the residence rules that apply to you.

      Also discuss insurance for your belongings (page in Dutch). Have it recorded which route and which work the agreement covers, including any storage. Read the quote and its terms and conditions before you confirm the move.

      Packing for abroad

      Well packed for your international move

      For your move to Germany, we recommend letting us pack your belongings. A few personal boxes are fine to do yourself. Our movers pack china, furniture and other fragile items with care.

      Do it yourself

      A few personal boxes

      • You can pack a few boxes of clothes and personal items yourself. Agree with us in advance what you want to do yourself.

      • Tell us what is in your own boxes. De Bresser adds the contents to the inventory list, numbers everything and gives you a copy.

      • Let us pack crockery, glassware and items that need extra protection. See our packing tips (in Dutch) for boxes you pack yourself.

      • Keep your passport, medication, keys and things for the first few days separate. Do not put them in with the belongings we take.

      Prepare your packing

      Our advice

      We pack your belongings

      • We protect your belongings all round with bubble wrap, corrugated cardboard and packing paper. We match the material to the item.

      • Edges and corners of furniture get extra protection. We fill empty spaces with packing paper, so items have less room to move inside the packaging.

      • For fragile or unusually shaped items, we arrange custom crates where needed. When loading, we make sure your belongings are well protected in the removal van.

      • Together we check if you are taking any special items that are subject to extra rules. We discuss those before the boxes are sealed.

      What details are needed for a quote?

      Give us both addresses, the volume of your belongings and the dates you have in mind. Mention any help with packing, assembly or temporary storage. If you add a large wardrobe or extra boxes after the survey, let us know so we can review the quote and schedule.

      Who arranges a spot for the removal van?

      That depends on your German municipality and where the van needs to park. Agree in advance who arranges any permission and signs. The decision lies with the competent authority. See the local examples from Berlin and Düsseldorf.

      Can I pack my own things?

      You can pack a few personal boxes yourself. For the rest of your belongings, we recommend leaving the packing to us. Agree which boxes and fragile items our team prepares when you book the packing service. That way it is clear before moving day who does what.

      What if my new home is not available yet?

      Does your old home have to be empty before the German one is ready? Discuss a later delivery and temporary storage in the Netherlands. Say which things you need in the meantime.

      How do I arrange insurance for Germany?

      Check which insurance suits your move to Germany, then talk through the insured amount, possible exclusions and what applies if your belongings are stored before delivery.

      Can I also move from Germany to the Netherlands?

      Moving the other way, from Germany to the Netherlands? Then discuss the German departure address and your destination in the Netherlands. The page on moving to the Netherlands explains how to prepare.
