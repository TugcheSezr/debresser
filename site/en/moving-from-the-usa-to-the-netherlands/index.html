      From the States

      Plan the whole move while you still live in the US

      Moving to the Netherlands from America does not mean flying over for a quote. Give us a room-by-room tour in a video survey, garage and attic included, and point out what you will leave behind and anything fragile.

      Send your US address with city, state and ZIP code, and your Dutch destination. An apartment with narrow stairs is a different job from a house with a drive, so add photos of the entrance.

      As you are moving to the Netherlands from the US, your belongings come from outside the EU and Dutch import rules for non-EU goods apply. Read more about sea shipments on our page about an overseas move (in Dutch).

      Arrival paperwork

      Paperwork for moving from the USA to the Netherlands

      Moving to Europe from the US in stages, living in another EU country first? Household goods you then move here from that country need no customs declaration, though alcohol, tobacco and vehicles can follow other rules. See moving within the EU (in Dutch).

      If you are moving from the US to the Netherlands, De Bresser prepares the inventory list, as with any move across a border. Each item gets a number and each box a note of its contents; if you pack boxes yourself, tell us what is inside. Your copy lets you check everything off on arrival, and customs can see the list when required.

      Coming from the US means an import declaration, and to import tax-free it must be lodged through a moving company or carrier. De Bresser takes care of the customs formalities that apply; your part is the information and paperwork. You sign the list we draw up and show proof that you now live here. Check the documents required by Dutch Customs (in Dutch).

      Staying longer than four months? Register with your municipality within five days of arrival; book the appointment from the US and ask which documents you need. US citizens need no MVV entry visa, but do need a residence permit beyond 90 days (see ind.nl). Lived here before? See the checklist for moving back (in Dutch).

      For your move

      Tax-free import is not a given

      Customs does not grant relief by default. They look at where you lived, how you used your goods and when you import them. See the rules for removal goods (in Dutch).

      Check which proof you can show

      Not on the municipal register yet when your shipment is cleared? Dutch Customs names other documents that show you have settled here. Find out which apply to you.

      Flag unusual items early

      Bringing a car, artwork or plants? Each may be checked more closely. List them, and any recent US purchases, in your request and look up the import rules before packing.

      Import rules for US households

      Your goods, your car and your pet each follow their own rules

      When your shipment from the US is cleared in the Netherlands, your removal company lodges the import declaration with a relief code. Customs then decides whether your household effects come in free of import duties. A car and a dog or cat are handled separately.

      Photo: Oude Turfmarkt 141-147 by Massimo Catarinella, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0. Edited for this page: background removed with AI, cropped, and a seagull and a wire retouched out. The edited image is also licensed under CC BY-SA 3.0. Download the edited image.

      Official sources checked on 21 October 2026. Which rules apply depends on your situation and what you ship.

      • Twelve months outside the EU, six months of use

        Household effects can only come in free of import duties if you move your normal residence to the EU after at least twelve consecutive months outside it, for example in the US. Each item needs six months of ownership and use, and you must keep using it in your new home. The goods must be imported within twelve months of taking up residence, and for a year after import they may not be lent, pledged, leased out or transferred. Alcohol, tobacco and commercial vehicles, among others, are excluded.

        Dutch Customs: household effects from outside the EU

      • Bringing your American car

        Your car needs its own import declaration, with copies of its registration and proof of ownership. Once you are registered in the Netherlands, take the import document from Customs, no more than 12 months old, to the RDW inspection in person; the car is checked against European requirements. If it qualifies as household effects, you pay no BPM, the Dutch car tax, but you then file a paper BPM return.

        Netherlands Worldwide: importing your car

      • Dogs and cats arriving from the US

        These rules apply if your dog or cat travels within five days of you; otherwise commercial rules apply, with arrival only via Schiphol. Your pet must be at least 15 weeks old and microchipped no later than its rabies vaccination, with a first vaccination given at least 21 days before entry. Bring an official veterinary health certificate, valid for 10 days from issue, and an owner's declaration. Coming from the low-risk US, not via a high-risk country, it needs no blood test. Report your pet to Customs on arrival.

        NVWA: dogs and cats from outside the EU
